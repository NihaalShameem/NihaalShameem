Descriptive Statistics

Before you can predict, test, or model anything, you have to describe it. This module is the vocabulary of data: how to summarize a column of numbers so it tells the truth — and why the "average" deposit is one of the most misleading numbers in iGaming.

🎯 What you'll be able to do

  • Explain population vs. sample and why the distinction matters.
  • Compute and interpret mean, median, and mode — and know which to trust.
  • Measure spread with range, variance, standard deviation, and IQR.
  • Use percentiles to describe whales, casual players, and everything between.
  • Read the shape of a distribution (skew) from a histogram.
  • Do all of the above in both SQL and Python, segmented by player cohort.

1.1  Population vs. sample

A population is every member of the group you care about — e.g. all players who ever registered. A sample is a subset you actually measure — e.g. last week's signups. Almost everything in statistics exists to answer one question: what can a sample tell me about the population it came from?

It matters for a practical reason you'll meet in Module 4: the formula for variance is slightly different for a sample (divide by n − 1) versus a population (divide by n). For now, just know our four tables are the population of this little operator — but we'll often treat slices of it as samples.

🎰 In iGaming terms
"What's the average deposit of all players this year?" is a population question. "Based on the first 200 signups from this new affiliate, what deposit size should I expect?" is a sample question — and it comes with uncertainty.

1.2  Central tendency: mean, median, mode

These three answer "what's a typical value?" — but they disagree, and the disagreement is the insight.

MeasureWhat it isBreaks when…
MeanSum ÷ count — the balance pointThere are outliers (whales)
MedianThe middle value when sortedRarely — it's robust
ModeThe most frequent valueData is continuous / all unique
∑ The mean
x̄ = (x₁ + x₂ + … + xₙ) / n = (1/n) · Σ xᵢ
Read "x-bar equals the sum of all the x's divided by how many there are."

Let's compute all three for deposit amounts. Start in SQL. Notice SQLite (what runs in your browser) has no built-in MEDIAN, so we use a portable sort-and-pick trick — a genuinely useful thing to know.

-- MEAN is easy: SELECT ROUND(AVG(amount), 2) AS mean_deposit FROM deposits; -- MEDIAN (portable SQLite trick: average the 1 or 2 middle rows): SELECT ROUND(AVG(amount), 2) AS median_deposit FROM ( SELECT amount FROM deposits ORDER BY amount LIMIT 2 - (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM deposits) % 2 OFFSET (SELECT (COUNT(*) - 1) / 2 FROM deposits) ); -- MODE (most common deposit method — mode shines on categories): SELECT method, COUNT(*) AS n FROM deposits GROUP BY method ORDER BY n DESC LIMIT 1;
💡 The big reveal
The mean deposit is about $73, but the median is about $41. The mean is nearly 80% higher. That gap is the fingerprint of a right-skewed distribution: a handful of whales drag the average up, so most players deposit far less than the "average." Report the median to stakeholders when you want a number that describes a typical player.

The same three measures in Python are one line each. pandas' .describe() is the fastest way to get oriented with any numeric column.

amt = deposits["amount"] print("mean: ", round(amt.mean(), 2)) print("median: ", round(amt.median(), 2)) print("mode: ", deposits["method"].mode()[0]) print() print(amt.describe().round(2))

1.3  Spread: range, variance, standard deviation

Two products can have the same mean GGR per bet and feel completely different to the business — one steady, one wildly swingy. Spread measures that swing. Standard deviation is the one you'll quote most: roughly, the typical distance of a value from the mean, in the same units as the data (dollars, minutes, etc.).

∑ Variance & standard deviation
variance σ² = (1/n) · Σ (xᵢ − x̄)² std dev σ = √(σ²)
A handy algebraic identity lets you compute it in one pass: σ² = E[x²] − (E[x])² — the "mean of the squares minus the square of the mean." That's exactly what we use in SQLite below.
🎰 Standard SQL vs. our sandbox
In Postgres / BigQuery / Snowflake you'd just write STDDEV(amount) and VAR_SAMP(amount). Browser SQLite has neither, so below we compute variance by hand. Learning the manual version is worth it — it's the same trick interviewers love to ask about.
SELECT ROUND(MIN(amount), 2) AS min_amt, ROUND(MAX(amount), 2) AS max_amt, ROUND(MAX(amount) - MIN(amount), 2) AS range_amt, ROUND(AVG(amount), 2) AS mean_amt, ROUND(AVG(amount*amount) - AVG(amount)*AVG(amount), 2) AS variance, ROUND(SQRT(AVG(amount*amount) - AVG(amount)*AVG(amount)), 2) AS std_dev FROM deposits;

In Python, .std() defaults to the sample standard deviation (the n − 1 version, called ddof=1). Pass ddof=0 for the population version that matches the SQL above. The difference is tiny on 800+ rows, but it's exactly the kind of detail that separates a senior analyst from a junior one.

amt = deposits["amount"] print("range: ", round(amt.max() - amt.min(), 2)) print("variance (pop): ", round(amt.var(ddof=0), 2)) print("std (sample): ", round(amt.std(ddof=1), 2)) # pandas default print("std (pop): ", round(amt.std(ddof=0), 2)) # matches SQL

1.4  Percentiles & quantiles

The median is just the 50th percentile. Percentiles let you describe any point in the distribution: the p90 deposit is the amount that 90% of deposits fall at or below. In iGaming this is how you define VIP thresholds, set responsible-gaming alerts, and size bonus offers without overpaying the masses.

Browser SQLite has no PERCENTILE_CONT either, but window functions give us a clean equivalent with PERCENT_RANK().

WITH ranked AS ( SELECT amount, PERCENT_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY amount) AS pr FROM deposits ) SELECT (SELECT MIN(amount) FROM ranked WHERE pr >= 0.50) AS p50, (SELECT MIN(amount) FROM ranked WHERE pr >= 0.90) AS p90, (SELECT MIN(amount) FROM ranked WHERE pr >= 0.99) AS p99;
amt = deposits["amount"] print(amt.quantile([0.50, 0.90, 0.95, 0.99]).round(2)) print() # A VIP rule of thumb: top 10% of depositors by amount vip_cut = amt.quantile(0.90) print(f"p90 deposit threshold: ${vip_cut:.2f}") print(f"deposits at/above it: {(amt >= vip_cut).mean()*100:.1f}% of all deposits")
🧠 Intuition: IQR & outliers
The Interquartile Range (IQR) = p75 − p25 is the spread of the "middle half" of players, and it's immune to whales. A common outlier rule flags anything above p75 + 1.5 × IQR. That's exactly how a box-and-whisker plot decides which deposits are dots on the far right.

1.5  Shape: skew & histograms

A single picture settles the mean-vs-median debate instantly. A histogram buckets values and counts them, revealing the shape. Money in iGaming is almost always right-skewed (a long tail of big spenders). Let's see it.

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt amt = deposits["amount"] fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8, 4)) ax.hist(amt[amt < 400], bins=40, color="#36bcf7", edgecolor="white") ax.axvline(amt.mean(), color="#f85149", lw=2, label=f"mean ${amt.mean():.0f}") ax.axvline(amt.median(), color="#3fb950", lw=2, label=f"median ${amt.median():.0f}") ax.set_title("Deposit amounts are right-skewed") ax.set_xlabel("deposit amount ($)"); ax.set_ylabel("count") ax.legend() plt.show()
💡 Read the chart
The green median line sits to the left of the red mean line. That ordering — median < mean — is the definition of right (positive) skew. Whenever you see it in a revenue or deposit column, reach for the median (or a log scale) when you summarize for stakeholders.

1.6  Descriptive stats by segment

Real analysis is rarely one number for everyone — it's the same number split by a dimension: value tier, acquisition channel, country, device. This is the GROUP BY in SQL and .groupby() in pandas, and it's where descriptive statistics start driving decisions.

SELECT p.value_tier, COUNT(DISTINCT d.player_id) AS depositors, COUNT(*) AS num_deposits, ROUND(AVG(d.amount), 2) AS avg_deposit, ROUND(SUM(d.amount), 0) AS total_deposited FROM deposits d JOIN players p ON p.player_id = d.player_id GROUP BY p.value_tier ORDER BY total_deposited DESC;
# Attach each deposit to its player's tier, then summarize d = deposits.merge(players[["player_id", "value_tier"]], on="player_id") summary = (d.groupby("value_tier")["amount"] .agg(deposits="count", avg="mean", median="median", p90=lambda s: s.quantile(0.9), total="sum") .round(2) .sort_values("total", ascending=False)) print(summary)
🎰 So what?
A tiny "Whale" segment usually contributes a wildly outsized share of total deposits. That single fact reshapes strategy: VIP retention is worth more than squeezing casual conversion, and a churned whale is a five-alarm fire. Descriptive stats, segmented, is how you find that lever before anyone asks.

🧠 Key takeaways
  • Mean ≠ median on skewed data — and iGaming money is always skewed.
  • Standard deviation tells you volatility in real units; IQR does it robustly.
  • Percentiles are the language of thresholds (VIP, alerts, bonuses).
  • Every summary gets sharper when you segment it.
  • SQL and pandas give the same answers — pick the tool that fits the workflow.
